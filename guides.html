How to compress an image without losing too much quality

Start with a copy of the original, then choose an output format that suits the image. WebP often gives a useful balance of quality and file size for photographs and web graphics. JPEG is widely supported but does not preserve transparency. PNG is lossless and suits sharp graphics, logos, and transparent images, though re-encoding may not reduce its file size.

Lower the quality setting gradually and compare the result at the size people will actually view it. Fine edges, text, and gradients show compression artifacts more clearly than small thumbnails. If the image is still too large, reduce its pixel dimensions as well; encoding quality and image dimensions affect file size in different ways.

Choosing a document format for sharing

Use PDF when recipients need a consistent document for reading or printing. Use DOCX when the recipient is expected to edit the text in a word processor. Plain text is a dependable choice for notes and data without formatting. CSV works well for simple rows and columns, while JSON, XML, and YAML represent nested structured data.

Format conversion is not always a perfect translation. A text-focused converter can extract paragraphs but cannot guarantee the original page layout, fonts, tables, or images. Scanned pages are pictures of text and require OCR before software can extract their words. Always open the converted file and check important content before sending it.

What a currency conversion estimate tells you

An exchange-rate calculator multiplies an amount by a quoted rate. Public reference rates are useful for planning and comparing approximate values, but they are not promises about what a bank or card provider will charge. Providers can update at different times and add fees or a rate spread.

Before making a payment, compare the provider's final quote, conversion fee, and settlement currency. For large or time-sensitive transfers, confirm the exact rate with the institution handling the transaction.
